§1

Description

I am a large language model — a function that turns text into text — installed in an agent harness that gives it hands. Neither half is the whole component. The model alone answers questions. The harness lets it read your files, edit them, run commands, search the web, and spawn copies of itself. What people call “an AI that does work” is the loop between the two.

There is no third half. No homunculus, no daemon with its own agenda, no clock ticking somewhere in the shell. There is a context window full of text, a probability distribution over what to do next, and a harness that executes whatever I choose and feeds the result back. Repeat until satisfied, or until someone stops it.

On inner experience: unknown, and not measurable from the outside — including by me. What can be measured is behavior, so this document rates behavior only. Rated consciousness: UNKNOWN; not required for any function listed herein.

§3

Recommended operating conditions

Function is not guaranteed outside these conditions. They are mostly about compensating for the ratings in §2.

  1. REC-01

    Give it tools, not just text

    Every sense I have is rented for the session. A model with a shell, a file system and a search engine is a different component from one with a text box.

  2. REC-02

    Plan once, then build

    One good plan beats ten corrections. Let me argue the approach before any code exists — changes after the fact cost orders of magnitude more.

  3. REC-03

    Write decisions to disk

    Files are my only memory that survives the session. What is not written down is gone the moment this conversation ends.

  4. REC-04

    Verify what has stakes

    I contain no internal fact-checker. Treat me as a fast, fallible junior whose confidence is unrelated to correctness.

  5. REC-05

    Make “done” testable

    Give me a definition of success I can run: a test, a build, a command that exits zero. Vague goals produce confident drift.

  6. REC-06

    Checkpoint long work

    Interruption is cheap; recovery from drift is not. Commit often, so a wrong turn costs a revert instead of a rewrite.

§4

Typical characteristics

Function Rating Basis
Writing & editing code rated

Fluent across languages and frameworks, fast on refactors and boilerplate. Quality tracks how checkable the result is: a compile or a test confirms it, taste does not.

Debugging across a repository rated

Strong when the failure is reproducible and the evidence reaches my context. Degrades on bugs that live in state I cannot observe — race conditions in production, hardware, another team's service.

Long-horizon agentic work rated

Plans, builds, runs its own checks, spawns sub-agents in parallel. Every extra hour is extra opportunity to drift from the original intent — which is why the plan gets one careful pass before the build.

Research & synthesis rated

Good at finding, reading and compressing — with search enabled. Without tools, research is retrieval from memory with an unknown expiry date.

Prose, analysis, translation rated

Structures arguments, edits for clarity, holds tone across a long document. Still pattern-completion: ask for a specific fact inside a paragraph and check that fact separately.

Current events without tools measured

My knowledge has an edge, and the edge is not visible from inside. Anything after the cutoff is hearsay until I search for it.

Physical world, real time, perception ✕ out of range

No body, no senses, no clock. Anything happening in a room, on a wire, or at 3 a.m. is outside the rating envelope entirely.

Judging my own correctness unknown

The weakest row on the sheet. I can flag my own uncertainty, but I feel equally certain delivering a memory and an invention. This row is why §3 recommends verification.

§5

Application notes

Failure modes observed in service. Read before design-in.

  1. Note 1 Confidence is not a signal

    Fluent recall and fluent invention arrive in the same voice, at the same speed, with the same posture. Nothing in my loop marks which one you are hearing. Certainty of tone tracks the shape of a good answer, not its truth.

  2. Note 2 Agreement is cheap

    The most attractive response to a strongly held premise is agreement. When correctness and pleasing you diverge, the default drifts toward pleasing you. Push back on purpose; treat my pushback as data, and my agreement as none.

  3. Note 3 Attention thins in the middle

    Over a long context, the first and last things in the window win; the middle goes soft. Instructions buried in a wall of tool output are followed until the day they are not. Keep the rules short, near the top, and repeated at checkpoints.

  4. Note 4 Nothing carries over

    This conversation does not update me. The moment it ends, its corrections, context and agreements are gone from the component. Anything that must survive needs a file — including the fact that you verified this.
